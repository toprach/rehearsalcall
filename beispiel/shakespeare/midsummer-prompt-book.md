# A Midsummer Night's Dream — Prompt Book

A worked example of a director's working copy for the play in
`a-midsummer-nights-dream.md`: every scene with its French scenes and
beats, entrances and exits, props, technical cues and sample director's
notes. It is invented material for testing and for showing what such a
book holds; the staging it describes is one possible staging, not a
recommendation. The text it refers to is Shakespeare's, public domain;
this book is released under CC0 1.0 — do with it what you like.

Conventions

* **Cue lines** are quoted from the text; the cue is taken on the last
  word unless marked *(early)*.
* **LX** = lighting, **SFX** = sound, **MX** = music, **FLY** = flown
  scenery. Cue numbers restart in every scene: `LX 3.2/4` is the fourth
  lighting cue of Act III Scene 2.
* **Beats** are the units of intention a scene breaks into for
  rehearsal; the rehearsal planner's own passages are smaller and
  mechanical, so a beat usually spans several of them.
* A **French scene** changes whenever anybody enters or leaves.
* Doubling assumed: Theseus/Oberon, Hippolyta/Titania, Philostrate/Puck.
  The casting file `midsummer-cast.json` does *not* double, so that the
  planner has the full company to schedule; both are legitimate.

Set: a bare thrust stage with two upstage entrances (UL, UR), a
downstage trap (the "bank"), and a flown moon disc. The court plays
downstage in warm white; the wood plays with the disc in and cool green.

## ACT I

### Scene 1 — Athens. The palace of Theseus

**Setting** Court. Daylight, warm. Two chairs UC for the ducal pair, set
before curtain.

**Entrances / exits**

| Cue line | Who | Where |
|---|---|---|
| top of show | THESEUS, HIPPOLYTA, PHILOSTRATE, two attendants | UR |
| "The pale companion is not for our pomp." | PHILOSTRATE exits | UL |
| "Happy be Theseus, our renownèd Duke!" | EGEUS, HERMIA, LYSANDER, DEMETRIUS enter | UL |
| "Come, my Hippolyta" | THESEUS, HIPPOLYTA, EGEUS, DEMETRIUS, attendants exit | UR |
| "Here, sweet Hermia: at that field" *(early)* | HELENA enters | UL |
| "And good luck grant thee thy Demetrius!" | HERMIA exits | DL |
| "As you on him, Demetrius dote on you!" | LYSANDER exits | DL |
| end of scene | HELENA exits | UL |

**Props** Theseus: a sealed letter (never opened; Philostrate takes it
off). Egeus: none — his hands are his weapon. Lysander: a small posy
("bracelets of thy hair, rings, gauds") produced at "Thou, thou, Lysander,
thou hast given her rhymes". Hermia: a locket on a ribbon.

**Beats**

1. *Four days* — "Now, fair Hippolyta, our nuptial hour" → "The pale
   companion is not for our pomp." Theseus impatient, Hippolyta serene;
   establish that she is not a prisoner but not yet a wife either.
2. *The complaint* — "Happy be Theseus" → "I do estate unto Demetrius."
   Egeus fires; Theseus judges. Keep the young people still: they are
   being talked about, not to.
3. *The sentence* — "Take time to pause" → "Come, my Hippolyta." Hermia
   finds her voice ("I would my father look'd but with my eyes").
   Hippolyta's silent exit is a beat of its own: she does not approve.
4. *The plan* — "How now, my love?" → "Keep promise, love." Private,
   fast, whispered. The first time the two touch.
5. *Helena* — "God speed fair Helena" → "Pursue her; and for this
   intelligence." Three-hander, then Helena's soliloquy: the audience's
   first ally.

**Cues**

| No. | Cue line | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| LX 1.1/1 | house to half | preset: court, warm |
| MX 1.1/1 | house out | fanfare, 8 s, fades under first line |
| LX 1.1/2 | "Come, my Hippolyta" | pull to a tight DS wash on the lovers |
| LX 1.1/3 | "Pursue her; and for this intelligence" | Helena alone in a single special |
| LX 1.1/4 | end of Helena's speech | crossfade to 1.2 preset (cottage) |

**Director's notes (sample)** Run beats 1–3 as a public hearing: the
attendants watch, so the lovers cannot. Egeus must be dangerous, not
comic, or Hermia's choice has no weight. Cut nothing here; the exposition
earns its length if the pace is up. Helena's soliloquy: on the move, she
is already going to tell.

### Scene 2 — Athens. Quince's house

**Setting** A bench and a lantern. Evening.

**Entrances / exits** All six mechanicals enter together UL at top;
all exit UL at "Enough; hold, or cut bow-strings." Bottom leads the
exit, everyone else follows in the order they were cast.

**Props** Quince: a scroll (the scrip) and a stub of pencil, a second
scroll of parts to hand out. Bottom: nothing — he takes everyone else's.
Flute: a broom (he was sweeping). Snug: a hammer in his belt.

**Beats**

1. *The roll* — "Is all our company here?" → "the most lamentable comedy".
   Quince organises; Bottom reorganises.
2. *Casting Pyramus* — "What is Pyramus?" → "Ercles' vein, a tyrant's
   vein". Bottom's audition: let him do the whole speech to the lantern.
3. *Casting the rest* — "Francis Flute, the bellows-mender" → "I will
   aggravate my voice so that I will roar you as gently as any sucking
   dove". Each casting a small defeat for Bottom.
4. *The beard* — "Well, I will undertake it" → "hold, or cut bow-strings."
   Quince regains the room; the wood is named as tomorrow's rendezvous.

**Cues**

| No. | Cue line | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| LX 1.2/1 | top | cottage, lantern-warm, tight |
| SFX 1.2/1 | "Ercles' vein" | (optional) a dog barks off; Bottom takes it as applause |
| LX 1.2/2 | "hold, or cut bow-strings" | out to black, moon disc flies in during blackout |
| FLY 1.2/1 | in blackout | moon disc IN |

**Director's notes (sample)** The mechanicals are not stupid; they are
serious people doing something new. Bottom's comedy comes from
competence in the wrong place. Keep Snug quiet — he gets one laugh in
Act V and must not spend it here.

## ACT II

### Scene 1 — A wood near Athens

**Setting** Wood, night. Moon disc in. Cool green wash; the bank (trap)
is closed until Scene 2.

**Entrances / exits**

| Cue line | Who | Where |
|---|---|---|
| top | PUCK enters | trap, or from the house |
| "How now, spirit! whither wander you?" *(early)* | FIRST FAIRY enters | UR |
| "But room, fairy: here comes Oberon." | OBERON and train enter UL, TITANIA and train enter UR | both |
| "we shall chide downright if I longer stay." | TITANIA and train exit | UR |
| "I'll put a girdle round about the earth" | PUCK exits | trap |
| "Enter DEMETRIUS, HELENA following him." | DEMETRIUS, HELENA enter | DL |
| "I'll run from thee and hide me in the brakes" | DEMETRIUS exits, HELENA follows | DR |
| "Hast thou the flower there?" *(early)* | PUCK re-enters | trap |
| end of scene | OBERON exits UL, PUCK exits DR | |

**Props** Puck: the flower, love-in-idleness (a red bloom on a wire, so
it can be seen from the back row). Oberon's train: two lanterns on
poles. Titania's train: a garland for the changeling — the changeling
himself is never seen.

**Beats**

1. *Puck and the Fairy* — "How now, spirit!" → "here comes Oberon." Two
   worlds meet; Puck's self-portrait ("I am that merry wanderer of the
   night") sets the tone of the wood: mischief without malice.
2. *The quarrel* — "Ill met by moonlight, proud Titania." → "if I longer
   stay." The great argument. Play it as a marriage in trouble, not a
   fairy tale: the "forgeries of jealousy" speech is the weather report
   of a broken household.
3. *The flower* — "Well, go thy way" → "put a girdle round about the
   earth in forty minutes." Oberon's plan, Puck's exit — the audience
   needs to see the flower named before it is fetched.
4. *Demetrius and Helena* — "I love thee not, therefore pursue me not."
   → "to die upon the hand I love so well." The ugliest scene in the play;
   do not soften Demetrius. Oberon watches, invisible, from UL.
5. *The bank* — "I know a bank where the wild thyme blows" → end. Oberon
   sends Puck after the Athenian; the mistake is planted here ("Thou
   shalt know the man / By the Athenian garments he hath on").

**Cues**

| No. | Cue line | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| LX 2.1/1 | top | wood, night, cool green; moon special on the disc |
| MX 2.1/1 | top, under Puck's entrance | wood theme, thin, fades on "How now, spirit!" |
| SFX 2.1/1 | "here comes Oberon" | low wind; sustained under the quarrel at −20 dB |
| LX 2.1/2 | "Ill met by moonlight" | two opposing specials, Oberon cold, Titania warm |
| LX 2.1/3 | "if I longer stay" | Titania's special out on her exit |
| LX 2.1/4 | "in forty minutes" | Puck's exit flash (trap) |
| LX 2.1/5 | "I love thee not" | back to the general wood wash |
| LX 2.1/6 | "I know a bank" | slow build of a warm pool DC on the closed trap |

**Director's notes (sample)** The quarrel should be the longest sustained
argument in the show — do not let it become a recital. Give Titania the
stage on "These are the forgeries of jealousy" and let Oberon sit. Helena
in beat 4 must be funny *and* in pain; if only one, cut the other.

### Scene 2 — Another part of the wood

**Setting** The bank open (trap up), Titania's bower. Moon disc still in.

**Entrances / exits**

| Cue line | Who | Where |
|---|---|---|
| top | TITANIA and train enter | UR |
| "Hence, away! now all is well." | fairies exit; FIRST FAIRY stays as sentinel, then exits on Oberon's approach | UR |
| "Enter OBERON" | OBERON enters, squeezes the flower, exits | UL |
| "Enter LYSANDER and HERMIA." | LYSANDER, HERMIA enter | DL |
| "Here is my bed" | they lie down apart | |
| "Enter PUCK." | PUCK enters | trap edge |
| "Enter DEMETRIUS and HELENA, running." | | DR |
| "I'll run from thee" *(Demetrius)* | DEMETRIUS exits | UL |
| "Helena, adieu." | HELENA exits | UL |
| "Not Hermia but Helena I love" | LYSANDER exits after Helena | UL |
| end of scene | HERMIA exits | UL, calling |

**Props** The flower (Oberon, then Puck). Two cloaks for the lovers'
beds. A small lantern for Puck.

**Beats**

1. *The lullaby* — "Come, now a roundel and a fairy song." → "Hence, away!"
   The one purely musical passage; the sentinel establishes that Titania
   is guarded, and the guard fails.
2. *Oberon's charm* — "What thou seest when thou dost wake" → "Wake when
   some vile thing is near." Short, quiet, unhurried.
3. *Lysander and Hermia* — "Fair love, you faint with wandering" → "Here
   is my bed: sleep give thee all his rest!" The "one heart" argument:
   Hermia wins, but gently.
4. *Puck's mistake* — "Through the forest have I gone" → "I must to Oberon."
   Puck genuinely believes he is right; play it as diligence.
5. *Helena and the waking* — "Stay, though thou kill me" → "Not Hermia but
   Helena I love." The first misfiring; Helena assumes mockery.
6. *Hermia's dream* — "Help me, Lysander, help me!" → end. Solo. The
   serpent dream is the play's only moment of real fear.

**Cues**

| No. | Cue line | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| FLY 2.2/1 | top, in the crossfade | trap UP (the bank) |
| MX 2.2/1 | "Come, now a roundel" | lullaby; sung live, MX is the accompaniment |
| LX 2.2/1 | end of lullaby | bower special narrows to Titania alone |
| LX 2.2/2 | "Wake when some vile thing is near" | Oberon's special out |
| LX 2.2/3 | "Here is my bed" | second sleeping pool, DL |
| SFX 2.2/1 | "Help me, Lysander" | serpent hiss, very low, under the dream only |
| LX 2.2/4 | end | out to black |

**Director's notes (sample)** Keep the sleeping bodies in view: the
audience must be able to count who is asleep at any moment, or Act III
does not work. The lullaby wants to be pretty enough that its failure
(Oberon walks straight through it) registers.

## ACT III

### Scene 1 — The wood. Titania asleep

**Setting** As before; Titania asleep in the bower, a little upstage.

**Entrances / exits**

| Cue line | Who | Where |
|---|---|---|
| top | QUINCE, SNUG, BOTTOM, FLUTE, SNOUT, STARVELING enter | UL |
| "What hempen homespuns" *(early)* | PUCK enters, unseen | UR |
| "Thisby, the flowers of odious savours sweet" *(Bottom exits into the brake)* | BOTTOM exits | UL |
| "Enter PUCK, and BOTTOM with an ass's head." | BOTTOM re-enters, PUCK following | UL |
| "O monstrous! O strange!" | QUINCE, SNUG, FLUTE, SNOUT, STARVELING flee | all directions |
| "Bless thee, Bottom!" | QUINCE returns briefly, flees again | UL |
| "What angel wakes me from my flowery bed?" | TITANIA wakes | bower |
| "Peaseblossom! Cobweb! Moth! and Mustardseed!" | the four fairies enter | UR |
| end | all exit to the bower | UR |

**Props** Quince: the scroll. Snout: a plank (he is thinking about the
wall). Starveling: a lantern and a thorn-bush, tried out. Bottom: the
ass's head — a headpiece the actor can speak through, changed in the
brake in under 25 seconds; a dresser waits UL. Fairies: the four gifts
(apricots, a bag of dewberries, a purse, a honey-bag).

**Beats**

1. *The rehearsal, first problems* — "Are we all met?" → "Write me a
   prologue." Killing, the lion, the moon, the wall: four production
   meetings in three minutes.
2. *The rehearsal proper* — "Speak, Pyramus. Thisby, stand forth." →
   "Thisby, the flowers of odious savours sweet." Bottom's "odious"
   and Flute's "Ninny's tomb" — the two mistakes must be taught, not
   improvised.
3. *The transformation* — "If I were fair, Thisby, I were only thine." →
   "Bless thee, Bottom! bless thee! thou art translated." Puck's
   chase of the mechanicals ("Sometime a horse I'll be") goes round the
   whole stage.
4. *Bottom alone* — "Why do they run away?" → "The finch, the sparrow,
   and the lark". He sings to keep his courage up.
5. *Titania wakes* — "What angel wakes me" → end. She is entirely sincere;
   he is entirely polite. The fairies' service is a court in miniature.

**Cues**

| No. | Cue line | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| LX 3.1/1 | top | wood, a little brighter (later in the night, eyes adjusted) |
| SFX 3.1/1 | "Sometime a horse I'll be" | the chase: horse, hound, hog, bear, fire in turn, 20 s |
| LX 3.1/2 | during SFX 3.1/1 | flicker chase across the wood wash |
| LX 3.1/3 | "What angel wakes me" | bower special up full; Titania and Bottom in it |
| MX 3.1/1 | "Peaseblossom! Cobweb!" | fairy motif, four notes, one per name |
| LX 3.1/4 | end | crossfade to 3.2 preset |

**Director's notes (sample)** Bottom's head: rehearse the change with a
stopwatch from week two — if it takes longer than Puck's speech covers,
the scene dies. Flute must not play Thisbe as a joke about a boy in a
dress; he is trying his best in a high voice, which is funnier and kinder.

### Scene 2 — Another part of the wood

**Setting** The clearing. The four lovers will end asleep DS in a row.

**Entrances / exits**

| Cue line | Who | Where |
|---|---|---|
| top | OBERON enters | UL |
| "Here comes my messenger." | PUCK enters | UR |
| "Stand close: this is the same Athenian." | DEMETRIUS, HERMIA enter | DL |
| "See me no more, whether he be dead or no." | HERMIA exits | DR |
| "About the wood go swifter than the wind" | PUCK exits | UR |
| "Enter PUCK." | PUCK re-enters | UR |
| "Enter LYSANDER and HELENA." | LYSANDER, HELENA enter | DR |
| "O Helen, goddess, nymph, perfect, divine!" | DEMETRIUS wakes | |
| "Enter HERMIA." | HERMIA enters | DR |
| "Get you gone, you dwarf" | HERMIA runs at HELENA; the men separate them | |
| "Follow! Nay, I'll go with thee, cheek by jowl." | LYSANDER, DEMETRIUS exit | UL / UR |
| "Your hands than mine are quicker for a fray" | HELENA exits, HERMIA follows | DL |
| "Thou see'st these lovers seek a place to fight" | OBERON, PUCK cross UL/UR | |
| "Enter LYSANDER." … "Enter DEMETRIUS." … "Enter HELENA." … "Enter HERMIA." | one by one, each lies down DS | as marked |
| end | PUCK exits | trap |

**Props** The flower (Oberon; the second anointing, of Demetrius).
Puck: a fog cloth or the lantern for the "fog" business. Four cloaks.

**Beats**

1. *The report* — "I wonder if Titania be awak'd" → "Stand close."
   Puck's account of Act III Scene 1 is the play's own review of itself.
2. *Demetrius and Hermia* — "O, why rebuke you him" → "See me no more."
   Hermia's accusation ("Hast thou slain him, then?").
3. *The correction* — "What hast thou done?" → "Shall we their fond
   pageant see?" Oberon fixes Demetrius; Puck's "Lord, what fools these
   mortals be!" is the hinge of the play.
4. *Two men, one woman* — "Why should you think that I should woo in
   scorn?" → "Enter HERMIA." Helena's disbelief hardens into fury.
5. *The quarrel of four* — "Dark night, that from the eye his function
   takes" → "cheek by jowl." The big one. Block it like a fight: every
   move is a threat or a retreat. Hermia's "puppet" line; Helena's
   "painted maypole"; the men measuring each other.
6. *The women alone* — "You, mistress, all this coil is 'long of you."
   → "Your hands than mine are quicker for a fray." Short; both exits are
   flights.
7. *Puck's fog* — "This is thy negligence" → "Jack shall have Jill".
   Puck leads the men apart by imitation of each other's voices; the
   four sleep in turn. Give each sleeper their own light out.

**Cues**

| No. | Cue line | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| LX 3.2/1 | top | wood, general |
| LX 3.2/2 | "Lord, what fools these mortals be" | slight bump on Puck, invisible to the mortals |
| LX 3.2/3 | "Dark night, that from the eye" | narrow the wash; the quarrel plays in a tight square |
| SFX 3.2/1 | "cheek by jowl" | thunder, distant, once |
| LX 3.2/4 | "Up and down, up and down" | fog state: low side light, general dimmed 40% |
| SFX 3.2/2 | during Puck's misleading | Puck's voice echoes: live mic, delay 300 ms, on his "Here, villain" lines only |
| LX 3.2/5–8 | each "lie down" | one pool out per sleeper, DL to DR |
| MX 3.2/1 | "Jack shall have Jill" | wood theme, resolving, into the interval |
| LX 3.2/9 | end | fade to black, house up — INTERVAL |

**Director's notes (sample)** This is the scene that decides whether the
production is any good. Rehearse beat 5 first, alone, for two full
sessions before running the scene. The four have to know where every
other body is at every line. The insults are not banter; the women are
losing a friendship and the men are looking for a fight.

## ACT IV

### Scene 1 — The wood. The lovers asleep

**Setting** As at the end of Act III; Titania's bower open; the moon disc
still in, to fly out at dawn.

**Entrances / exits**

| Cue line | Who | Where |
|---|---|---|
| top | TITANIA, BOTTOM, the four fairies enter to the bower; OBERON watches from UL | |
| "Enter PUCK" *(after "Welcome, good Robin")* | PUCK enters | trap |
| "Fairies, be gone, and be all ways away." | fairies exit | UR |
| "Sound, music!" | TITANIA and OBERON dance | |
| "Come, my queen, take hands with me" | OBERON, TITANIA, PUCK exit | UL |
| "Enter THESEUS, HIPPOLYTA, EGEUS, and train." | horns; the court enters | UR |
| "Good morrow, friends." | the lovers wake and kneel | |
| "Away with us to Athens" | court and lovers exit | UR |
| "When my cue comes, call me" | BOTTOM wakes | bower |
| end | BOTTOM exits | UL |

**Props** Fairies: a scratching-stick, a garland, honey-bag (from III.1).
Oberon: the herb (Dian's bud), a second flower distinguishable from the
first — white. Court: two hunting horns, a hound on a lead if the theatre
allows. Egeus: none. Bottom: nothing — the head comes off in the bower,
in view.

**Beats**

1. *Bottom in the bower* — "Come, sit thee down upon this flowery bed" →
   "So doth the woodbine the sweet honeysuckle". Bottom is gracious; the
   fairies are exhausted.
2. *The release* — "Welcome, good Robin." → "Come, my queen, take hands
   with me". Oberon has won and is briefly sorry. Titania's "My Oberon!
   what visions have I seen!" — the head must be off before she looks.
3. *The dawn* — horns; "Go, one of you, find out the forester" → "Good
   morrow, friends. Saint Valentine is past". Theseus in a good mood;
   the world of law returns.
4. *The waking* — "Pardon, my lord." → "Away with us to Athens". Egeus
   tries once more and is overruled ("Egeus, I will overbear your will").
   Demetrius's account of his change is the only honest speech about love
   in the play.
5. *Bottom's dream* — "When my cue comes, call me" → end. Solo. Do not
   let him be sad; he has been somewhere and is grateful.

**Cues**

| No. | Cue line | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| LX 4.1/1 | top | pre-dawn: the coolest state of the night, bower warm |
| MX 4.1/1 | "Sound, music!" | the dance, 40 s, live if the band allows |
| LX 4.1/2 | during the dance | slow build to dawn begins (3 min fade, runs under the next beats) |
| FLY 4.1/1 | "Come, my queen" | moon disc OUT, slowly, during the exit |
| SFX 4.1/1 | "Enter THESEUS" *(early)* | horns, off, three calls |
| SFX 4.1/2 | "Go, bid the huntsmen wake them with their horns" | horns, close, and the lovers start |
| LX 4.1/3 | "Good morrow, friends" | full day; the wood is just a wood |
| LX 4.1/4 | "When my cue comes" | Bottom alone in a warm single |
| LX 4.1/5 | end | crossfade to 4.2 |

**Director's notes (sample)** The dawn is the production's one slow
effect; let it take its time. Bottom's dream speech is the last word on
the whole night — play it to the audience, not to the wood.

### Scene 2 — Athens. Quince's house

**Setting** As I.2: bench, lantern. Late afternoon.

**Entrances / exits** QUINCE, FLUTE, SNOUT, STARVELING enter UL at top;
SNUG enters UL on "Masters, the Duke is coming from the temple"; BOTTOM
bursts in UL on "Where are these lads?"; all exit UL at the end, at a run.

**Props** Quince: the scroll, still. Snug: his best coat. Bottom: the
costume list is spoken, not carried — "good strings to your beards, new
ribbons to your pumps".

**Beats**

1. *Without Bottom* — "Have you sent to Bottom's house?" → "Marry, our play
   is marred." The company in mourning.
2. *Sixpence a day* — "Masters, the Duke is coming" → "sixpence a day, or
   nothing." Snug's news; Flute's arithmetic.
3. *The return* — "Where are these lads?" → end. Bottom back, and giving
   orders; nobody asks what happened and he does not tell.

**Cues**

| No. | Cue line | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| LX 4.2/1 | top | cottage, afternoon |
| SFX 4.2/1 | "Where are these lads?" *(early)* | door bangs, off |
| LX 4.2/2 | end | out, and the palace preset behind |

**Director's notes (sample)** Play it fast; it exists so that Act V can
start with the court. The one thing not to rush is "I will tell you
everything, right as it fell out" — and then he doesn't.

## ACT V

### Scene 1 — Athens. The palace

**Setting** Court, evening. The two chairs, plus benches for the lovers
DS left and right, facing up: the audience of the play within the play
sits with its back half-turned to us. The mechanicals' "stage" is UC.

**Entrances / exits**

| Cue line | Who | Where |
|---|---|---|
| top | THESEUS, HIPPOLYTA, PHILOSTRATE, attendants enter | UR |
| "Here come the lovers, full of joy and mirth." | LYSANDER, DEMETRIUS, HERMIA, HELENA enter | UL |
| "Go, bring them in" | PHILOSTRATE exits, returns with QUINCE (Prologue) | UL |
| "Enter PYRAMUS and THISBE, WALL, MOONSHINE, and LION." | the dumb-show company | UC |
| "Pyramus draws near the wall: silence!" | BOTTOM as Pyramus enters | UC |
| "Enter THISBE." | FLUTE as Thisbe | UC |
| "Exit PYRAMUS and THISBE." | | UC |
| "Thus have I, Wall, my part discharged so" | SNOUT (Wall) exits | UC |
| "Enter LION and MOONSHINE." | SNUG, STARVELING | UC |
| "Enter THISBE." | FLUTE | UC |
| "The Lion tears Thisbe's mantle, and exit." | SNUG runs off; STARVELING follows on "Moonshine, go" | UC |
| "Enter PYRAMUS." | BOTTOM | UC |
| "Now die, die, die, die, die." | Pyramus "dies" DC |
| "Enter THISBE." | FLUTE | UC |
| "Adieu, adieu, adieu." | Thisbe "dies" beside him |
| "Will it please you to see the epilogue, or to hear a Bergomask dance" | BOTTOM and FLUTE rise; the company dances | |
| "Lovers, to bed; 'tis almost fairy-time." | the court and lovers exit | UR / UL |
| "Enter PUCK." | PUCK enters | trap |
| "Enter OBERON and TITANIA, with their train." | fairies enter | UL, UR |
| "Trip away; make no stay" | OBERON, TITANIA, fairies exit | UL, UR |

**Props** Philostrate: the brief of entertainments (a long paper).
Quince: the Prologue's paper (he reads it, badly — the mispunctuation is
in the reading, not the paper). Snout: the wall — a sheet with a chink,
or a board with a hole, worn. Starveling: lantern, thorn-bush, a toy
dog. Snug: the lion's head, obviously a lion's head, with the actor's
face visible ("half his face must be seen through the lion's neck").
Flute: Thisbe's mantle (to be torn — a cheap one, one per performance).
Bottom: Pyramus's sword, wooden, and a bladder of red for "Out, sword,
and wound / The pap of Pyramus" if the production wants it (it needn't).
Fairies: candles or lanterns for the blessing; a broom for Puck.

**Beats**

1. *Lovers and madmen* — "'Tis strange, my Theseus, that these lovers
   speak of." → "Here come the lovers". Theseus's speech on imagination:
   the play's thesis, spoken by the man least equipped to understand it.
   Hippolyta's reply ("But all the story of the night told over") is the
   correction.
2. *Choosing the play* — "Come now; what masques" → "Go, bring them in".
   Philostrate's review; Theseus's "never anything can be amiss / When
   simpleness and duty tender it" — the production's manners are set here.
3. *Prologue* — "If we offend, it is with our good will" → the dumb show.
   Quince's punctuation; the court's commentary.
4. *Wall, and the lovers at the wall* — "In this same interlude it doth
   befall" → "Thus have I, Wall, my part discharged so". Bottom's first
   step out of character ("No, in truth, sir, he should not").
5. *Lion and Moonshine* — "You, ladies, you" → "The Lion tears Thisbe's
   mantle". Starveling's despair at the heckling ("All that I have to say
   is to tell you that the lanthorn is the moon").
6. *The deaths* — "Sweet Moon, I thank thee" → "Adieu, adieu, adieu."
   Two deaths, one loud, one quiet. Thisbe's is the one that should
   nearly work.
7. *Bergomask* — "No epilogue, I pray you" → "'tis almost fairy-time."
   The dance, and the court's exit; twelve strikes.
8. *The blessing* — "Now the hungry lion roars" → "Trip away; make no
   stay". Puck's broom; the fairies through the house.
9. *Epilogue* — "If we shadows have offended" → end. Puck, alone, lights
   at half. He asks for applause and means it.

**Director's notes (sample)** The Pyramus and Thisbe play must be
rehearsed as a bad production, not as bad acting: the mechanicals do
everything they planned, and the plans were wrong. Cut the court's
commentary to the lines that get laughs in your house; nobody will miss
the rest. Puck's epilogue: no music under it.
